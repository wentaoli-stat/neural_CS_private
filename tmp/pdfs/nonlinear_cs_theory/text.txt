Nonlinear Aggregation of Composite Sub-scores:
The Godambe Gap and the Auxiliary-Parameter
Problem
Research note — theory and algorithms
July 9, 2026
Abstract
This note consolidates the theoretical results and the algorithms they justify. Part I states
the nonlinear Godambe gap: for non-exponential-family models the full Fisher score lies outside
the closed linear span of marginal and pairwise sub-scores, so nonlinear aggregation strictly
improves on every linear composite score — in the degrees-of-freedom direction, permanently
and at order one. This is the load-bearing novelty claim; the gap closes in both degenerate
limits (ν→∞ , ρ→ 0), and whether it is order-one in other parameter directions is model-
dependent and left open. Part II resolves the auxiliary-parameter problem : the surrogate
score must be evaluated at someβ, and amortized SBI forces β =bβ(X) to be data-dependent.
A single translation identity, forced by Fisher consistency, yields (i) an impossibility result
— no function of the score value alone can be first-order pilot-invariant without becoming
ancillary — and (ii) a complete characterization: on the pair ( bβ,Sψ,ϕ(X,bβ)) the first-order
pilot-invariant statistics are exactly the functions of the one-step estimatorbθ1. The degeneracy
of Sψ,ϕ(X,bθ1), and the vanishing of the score at the estimating-equation root, are corollaries
of the same identity. Part III separates validity from efficiency: in the SBI regime pilot error
never threatens posterior validity, only sharpness, and the conditions the pilot must satisfy
are tiered accordingly. Part IV derives the algorithms as consequences and maps each claim
to an experiment.

3 Part II: the auxiliary-parameter problem
The surrogate score is a two-argument object Sψ,ϕ(x,β ). Mode A uses (Section 5) query it on the
diagonal β = θ; Mode B needs a θ-free statistic, which forces β = bβ(X) to be data-dependent.
This section characterizes completely what can and cannot be done about the resulting pilot
dependence.

Remark 12 (Interpretation). Part (a) says: pilot-invariance and θ-informativeness are the same
derivative, with opposite sign. Trying to launder the pilot error out of the score value alone
necessarily launders out the signal. In particular there is no “debiased score” summary — a
natural reviewer suggestion — and no rescue from over-identification ( q > p): directions in the
left null space of H are pilot-insensitive precisely because they are θ-insensitive.
Part (b) says: the one-step estimator is not one option among several . It is the unique first-
order pilot-invariant coordinate on the pair, up to bijection. Any procedure that removes pilot
dependence while retaining information — including “improve bβ to ˜β using the surface, then
evaluateSψ,ϕ(X, ˜β)” — reconstructs bθ1 or fails.

4 Part III: validity, efficiency, and pilot conditions
Proposition 19 (Validity is unconditional; proved). Let bβ :X →Rp be any measurable map
— consistent, inconsistent, or arbitrary. Then T (X) = (bβ(X), Sψ,ϕ(X,bβ(X))) is a statistic of X
alone, and p(θ| T (Xobs)) is an exact posterior given T . Pilot error introduces no bias into SBI
inference.
Proof. Composition of measurable maps. The posterior given any statistic is well defined and
exact for that statistic; nothing about its construction need reference θ.
Remark 20 (The deployability pillar, stated correctly) . Proposition 19 is the precise content of
“pilot robustness”. SBI conditioning converts pilot error from a validity problem into an efficiency
problem. This is a categorical difference from the classical one-step regime, where a bad pilot
yields a wrong estimator and wrong sandwich intervals. The claim to make is therefore not “we
mitigate bad pilots” but “conditioning makes pilot error an efficiency question, and the tube
plus the invariant coordinate recover the efficiency.” The narrower phrase “the main obstacle to
amortizing score-based summaries” should be preferred to “. . . to using score-based summaries”,
since at a fixed evaluation point the obstacle largely disappears (Proposition 24).

is what imposes op(n−1/4).
4.1 Tiered pilot conditions
Remark 26 (Pilot design under heavy tails) . For Student-t with ν∈ (3, 4] the natural moment
pilot forν (sample kurtosis) requires a finite fourth moment and is inconsistent or arbitrarily slow.
Use rank- or quantile-based pilots: Kendall’s τ / Gaussian-rank correlation for ρ, a quantile-ratio
or tail-index estimator forν. Both are closed-form and√n-consistent without moment conditions.
This detail will be checked by any referee who knows t-models.

5 Part IV: algorithms
The organizing axis is the evaluation point β. Mode A queries the surface on the diagonal β =θ
(no pilot needed). Mode B needs a θ-free map, obtained by collapsing β: freeze it, retain both
coordinates, or Newton-correct it into the invariant coordinate.
