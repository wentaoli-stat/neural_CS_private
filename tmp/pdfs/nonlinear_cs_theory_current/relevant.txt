2.6 Motivating examples
The following are ordered as they should appear in the paper: two that establish (Q1), one that
establishes (Q2) and carries the headline, one negative control, and two that delimit scope.
Example 1 (Elliptical AR-scatter field — the (Q1) workhorse) . Y ∼ tν{0, Σ(ρ)} with Σ(ρ)−1
tridiagonal (AR(1) scatter). Level 1. Closed-form g0, so the gap is measurable against ground
truth. Theorem 8: nCS on lag- ≤ 1 pairwise sub-scores is exact; linear CS is not, with deficit a
fixed fraction of Θ(n) information in the ρ direction. Measured REA≈ 0.76–0.85 at ν = 3 versus
0.87–0.93 at ν = 10, both → 1 under the oracle — the ν-dependence is the distance from the
Gaussian fixed point. Must be labelled unambiguously as the elliptical model , never as
“Student-t AR(1)” (Remark 7). Report the ρ direction, not ν (Remark 9(c)).
Example 2 (Global regime mixture — exact-inversion illustration) . Yi| Z∼ (1−Z)f0 +Zf1
with a single global regime indicator. Level 1, Theorem 10(a). The recovery map is available in
closed form: ϕ(s) = log{1 + (1−π)s}/{1−πs} recovers logri exactly, giving oracle RE = 1.000.
Its value is pedagogical — it exhibits ϕ as the inverse of the sub-score’s saturation (Principle 2)
— and it is an honest illustration of Proposition 12: Method C reaches 0.98, so this example must
not be used to argue for pre-aggregation nonlinearity.
Example 3 (Independent block latents — the headline (Q2) example) . m i.i.d. blocks of size J,
each with its own latent: blockwise- t (per-block scale mixture), common-shock ( Yb,i = λb +εb,i,
d-dimensional), or max-stable replicates. Level 2, Theorem 14. This is the load-bearing example:
information Θ(m) with no saturation, deficit stable in m and tunable through J or d, exact
recovery by hierarchical per-block pooling and not by global pooling, and RE C ≈ REA so the
comparison isolates pre-aggregation nonlinearity. Measured: block- t 0.89→ 0.99; common shock
d=15 0.80→ 0.99; max-stable 0.78→ 1.000 (identical to four decimals between A and C).
Example 4 (Gaussian banded-precision field — negative control) . Same design as Example 1
with a Gaussian generator. By Theorem 8(ii) the gap is exactly zero, and by Proposition 6 any
first-order Markov model with a pairwise+marginal library is likewise exactly zero. A method that
beats the linear ceiling here is reporting noise. Two independent negative controls — generator
Gaussianity and Level-0 separability — are stronger than one.
Example 5 (Delay-k threshold AR — granularity, not nonlinearity). Yt =αYt−1+βYt−11{Yt−k >
c} +εt with delay k exceeding the library’s window. Level 2. With triple margins covering the
delay, linear CS is exact ( R2 = 1.000000, Proposition 6); with only pairwise margins, pointwise
ϕ gains essentially nothing (0 .892 → 0.900) while windowed ϕ at matched granularity gains
+0.09 (→ 0.98 against a triple-margin ceiling of 0 .99). The cleanest available demonstration of
Principle 1: the batching structure, not the flexibility of ϕ, is what buys recovery.
Example 6 (HMM / stochastic volatility — Level 3 and the window law) . Two-state mean-
or variance-switching HMM, or stochastic volatility with quadrature-exact bivariate sub-scores.
Level 3, Theorem 10(c): no finite exact recoverer, geometric window law 1 − RE≲Cρ2w. Mea-
sured on the persistence parameter: 0 .18 (pointwise)→ 0.67 (windows, b=2)→ 0.80 (with lags),
pointwise ϕ contributing +0.00. This example earns its place by supplying a rate rather than a
dichotomy, and by motivating recurrent or attention aggregation as the Level-3 completion. Pre-
dictions worth one confirmatory run: Hawkes processes are Level 3 with window scale (1−α/κ)−1,
so near-criticality plays the role of ρ↑ 1.
